==============================================
Assessment: AR project
==============================================

.. list-table::
   :widths: 30 70

   * - **Type**
     - Project
   * - **Dates**
     - Starts Term 1, Week 4. Due Term 1, Week 7. Ends Term 1, Week 8.
   * - **Software**
     - Adobe Photoshop and Assemblr Studio
   * - **Achievement standard**
     - Students design the user experience of a digital system.
   * - **Capabilities**
     - Critical and creative thinking: repurpose existing ideas to meet needs in new contexts; represent ideas and
       justify thinking; evaluate strategies. Personal and social: use feedback to identify achievements and
       areas for improvement.

Learning intentions
--------------------

* I can design the user experience of an AR experience for a real audience.
* I can create my own image assets in Photoshop using selections, layers, masks and text.
* I can build an interactive AR experience in Assemblr Studio and improve it using feedback.

The task
---------

Create an **augmented reality experience** using images you have created or edited in Photoshop.

Your experience must **teach, guide or entertain** a specific audience. Choose one of these briefs or
suggest your own to your teacher:

* **Welcome to our school:** an AR guide for new Year 7 students (no real student faces or names).
* **Science in 3D:** explain a science idea (for example, the water cycle, the solar system or a food chain).
* **AR museum:** bring an animal, invention or historical object to life with facts.
* **AR story:** a short interactive story for primary school children where taps decide what happens.
* **AR product:** an advertisement for an invented product that shows how it works.

Requirements
~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Your AR experience must include:

* at least **five** PNG assets that **you** created or edited in Photoshop (with transparent backgrounds)
* evidence of **selections**, **layers**, **masks** and **text** in your PSD files
* at least **two scenes**
* at least **three different types** of interactivity (for example, Rotate, Visibility and Jump Up)
* clear instructions for the user (for example, "Tap the volcano!")
* a published project with a **QR marker** or link that works on a tablet

.. admonition:: Use images responsibly
   :class: warning

   Only use photos you took yourself, images you drew, or images with a licence that allows reuse (for example,
   Creative Commons or images your teacher provides). Do not use logos, characters or images from games, movies
   or brands. Do not include photos of other people without their permission. Use only your first name.

Stages
-------

Stage 1: Define and design (Week 4)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

#. Choose your brief and describe your **audience**.
#. Survey or interview at least **three** people from (or like) your audience to find out what they need.
#. Write at least **three functional** and **two non-functional** requirements.
#. Sketch **two different** storyboard ideas. Compare them against your design criteria and choose one.

Use the :download:`AR project planning template <files/ar_project_planning_template.docx>`.

Stage 2: Create assets in Photoshop (Weeks 5–6)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

#. Create or photograph your images.
#. Use **selection tools** and **layer masks** to cut out objects.
#. Organise your PSD with **clearly named layers** and groups.
#. Add **text** (titles, labels, instructions) with suitable fonts.
#. Export each asset as a **PNG** with a transparent background.

Stage 3: Build in Assemblr (Weeks 6–7)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

#. Upload your PNGs and arrange them in 3D across at least two scenes.
#. Add at least three types of interactivity with sensible start times and durations.
#. Preview often and fix problems.

Stage 4: Test, improve and publish (Week 7)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

#. Ask at least **two** people to test your experience on a tablet **without help**. Record their feedback.
#. Make at least **two** improvements based on the feedback.
#. Publish your project and download the QR marker.

Stage 5: Evaluate (Week 7)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Write a short evaluation (150–250 words) answering:

#. How well does your AR experience meet each of your requirements?
#. What feedback did you get, and what did you change because of it?
#. Which Photoshop or Assemblr skill are you proudest of, and why?
#. What would you improve if you had more time?

What to submit
---------------

Submit these through the school's online learning platform by the due date:

#. Your completed **planning template** (audience, survey results, requirements, two storyboards, comparison).
#. Your **PSD file(s)**, or screenshots of your Layers panel showing selections, layers, masks and text.
#. Your **PNG asset** folder (zipped).
#. The **link** to your published Assemblr project and the **QR marker** image.
#. A **screen recording** (30–90 seconds) of someone using your AR experience on a tablet.
#. Your **testing record** and **evaluation**.

Marking guide
--------------

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 16 21 21 21 21

   * - Criterion
     - Excellent (4–5)
     - Good (3)
     - Satisfactory (2)
     - Needs work (0–1)
   * - **Design and planning** (VC2TDI10C01, C03)
     - Audience needs clearly identified from survey or interview data. Clear functional and non-functional
       requirements. Two detailed storyboards compared against criteria, with a justified choice.
     - Audience needs and requirements identified. Two storyboards with a simple comparison.
     - Some requirements. One or two basic storyboards.
     - Little or no planning.
   * - **Photoshop assets** (selections, layers, masks, text)
     - Five or more high-quality assets with clean edges. Skilful use of selections, masks, named layers and
       well-chosen text. All exported correctly as transparent PNGs.
     - Five assets with mostly clean edges. Selections, layers, masks and text used correctly.
     - Some assets with rough edges. Some tools used. Most PNGs transparent.
     - Few assets. Little evidence of the required tools.
   * - **AR build** (arrangement and scenes)
     - Objects arranged thoughtfully in 3D at different depths and scales. Two or more well-organised scenes.
     - Objects arranged sensibly. Two scenes.
     - Objects placed but the scene looks flat or cluttered. One or two scenes.
     - Incomplete build.
   * - **Interactivity and user experience** (VC2TDI10D05)
     - Three or more interactivity types that work smoothly together and suit the purpose. Clear instructions.
       Easy and engaging to use.
     - Three interactivity types that work. Users can mostly work out what to do.
     - One or two interactivity types. Some confusion for users.
     - Interactivity missing or not working.
   * - **Testing, improvement and evaluation**
     - Feedback from two or more testers recorded and used to make clear improvements. Insightful evaluation
       against every requirement.
     - Feedback recorded and at least one improvement made. Evaluation covers most requirements.
     - Some testing. Brief evaluation.
     - No testing or evaluation.

**Total: 25 marks** (5 criteria × 5 marks)

.. note::

   Grades are reported from A+ to NP. A task below a C standard must be resubmitted. Your teacher will confirm how
   the marks convert to grades.

Exemplar ideas
---------------

* **Water cycle (two scenes):** Scene 1 has a sun, ocean, cloud and mountain cut out from photos. Tapping the
  sun makes water droplets (PNG) **move** up to the cloud, and the cloud **scales out**. A "Next" button
  **switches** to Scene 2, where tapping the cloud **shows** rain and plays a rain **sound**.
* **Welcome to the lab:** a robot (made from layered shapes in Photoshop) **waves** its arm when tapped, says
  hello and **shows** three lab safety rules one by one. A button opens a second scene with a lab equipment quiz.

Exam-style question
--------------------

.. structuredquestion:: Evaluating an AR project
   :total-marks: 5
   :category: AR project

   .. stimulus::

      Jai's AR project teaches Year 5 students about volcanoes. During testing, two students did not know they could
      tap the volcano, and one said the fact labels were too small to read.

   .. subquestion:: a) Suggest two improvements Jai could make based on this feedback.
      :marks: 2

      .. model-answer::

         * Add a large "Tap the volcano!" label or a glowing arrow pointing to the volcano.
         * Make the fact labels bigger, with bold text and a strong contrast background, then export them again.

      .. marking-guidance::

         1 mark for each improvement that responds directly to the feedback (maximum 2).

   .. subquestion:: b) Explain why testing with Year 5 students was more useful than testing with Jai's classmates.
      :marks: 2

      .. model-answer::

         Year 5 students are the real audience, so their feedback shows whether the experience suits their age and
         reading level. Jai's classmates already know how Assemblr works and might not notice the same problems.

      .. marking-guidance::

         * 1 mark for identifying Year 5 students as the target audience.
         * 1 mark for explaining why classmates might not find the same problems.

   .. subquestion:: c) Name one non-functional requirement that the feedback shows was not met.
      :marks: 1

      .. model-answer::

         The experience must be easy for Year 5 students to use without help (or: text must be readable on a tablet).

      .. marking-guidance::

         1 mark for a non-functional requirement about ease of use or readability.
