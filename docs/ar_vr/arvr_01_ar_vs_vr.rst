=============================================
1. Augmented reality vs virtual reality
=============================================

Learning intentions
--------------------

* I can explain what virtual reality (VR) and augmented reality (AR) are.
* I can describe the differences between VR, AR and mixed reality (MR).
* I can describe everyday uses of AR and VR and use the correct terms.

Success criteria
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* I can sort examples into VR, AR and MR.
* I can explain at least five AR/VR key terms.
* I can create a concept map that links AR and VR to their uses, devices and key terms.

The reality spectrum
---------------------

Think of a line. At one end is the **real world**, with no technology added. At the other end is a
completely **virtual world** made by a computer. Everything in between is called **extended reality (XR)**.

.. code-block:: text

   REAL WORLD ──── Augmented reality ──── Mixed reality ──── Virtual reality ──── VIRTUAL WORLD
   (no tech)       (digital on top of     (digital objects   (fully computer-
                    the real world)        interact with      made world)
                                           the real world)

Virtual reality (VR)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

**Virtual reality** replaces the real world with a completely computer-generated world. You usually
wear a **headset** that covers your eyes, so you can only see the virtual world. When you turn your
head, the view changes, which makes you feel like you are really there. This feeling is called
**immersion** or **presence**.

* **Devices:** VR headsets (standalone headsets or headsets connected to a computer or console), hand
  controllers.
* **Examples:** VR games, virtual tours of museums, flight simulators, surgery training.

Augmented reality (AR)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

**Augmented reality** adds digital content, such as images, 3D models, text or sound, **on top of**
your view of the real world. You can still see everything around you. *Augment* means "to add to".

* **Devices:** Phones and tablets (most common), smart glasses.
* **Examples:** Face filters on camera apps, Pokémon GO, apps that let you place furniture in your
  room before you buy it, maps that draw arrows on the street through your camera, AR textbooks.

Mixed reality (MR)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

**Mixed reality** goes further than AR. Digital objects understand the real world and **interact** with
it. For example, a virtual ball could bounce off your real table, or hide behind your real couch.

* **Devices:** Mixed reality headsets that use cameras to show the real world with digital objects
  blended in.

Comparing VR and AR
--------------------

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 25 37 38

   * - Feature
     - Virtual reality
     - Augmented reality
   * - What you see
     - Only a computer-made world
     - The real world with digital content added
   * - Usual device
     - Headset that blocks your view
     - Phone, tablet or smart glasses
   * - Awareness of surroundings
     - Low, so you need a safe, clear space
     - High, as you can still see around you
   * - Cost
     - Usually higher (needs a headset)
     - Usually lower (most people have a phone)
   * - Good for
     - Full immersion: games, simulations, training
     - Adding information to the real world: shopping, learning, navigation

How AR works
-------------

An AR app needs to know **where** to put the digital content. It uses the device's **camera**,
**sensors** and **software** to understand the scene.

* **Marker-based AR:** The app recognises a special image, such as a QR code or a poster (called a
  **marker** or **target**). The digital content appears on top of the marker.
* **Markerless AR:** The app finds flat surfaces, such as the floor or a table (called **plane detection**),
  and places the content there.
* **Location-based AR:** The app uses GPS to place content at real-world locations (like Pokémon GO).
* **Face tracking:** The app finds your face and tracks points on it, such as eyes and mouth, to add
  filters.

In this topic you will use **Assemblr Studio** to build your own AR experience. Assemblr can show your
project on top of a **QR or custom marker**, or around your real environment without a marker.

Key terms
----------

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 28 72

   * - Term
     - Meaning
   * - Extended reality (XR)
     - The umbrella term for AR, MR and VR
   * - Immersion
     - The feeling of being "inside" a digital experience
   * - Headset (HMD)
     - A head-mounted display worn over the eyes
   * - Marker (target)
     - An image the AR app recognises to know where to place content
   * - Markerless AR
     - AR that places content on surfaces without needing a marker
   * - Plane detection
     - Finding flat surfaces like floors and tables
   * - Tracking
     - Following the position and movement of the device, a face or a marker
   * - Field of view
     - How much of the scene you can see at one time
   * - 3D model
     - A digital object with width, height and depth
   * - Asset
     - Any file used in a project, such as an image, model or sound
   * - Interactivity
     - Content that responds to the user, for example moving when tapped

Everyday uses
--------------

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 20 40 40

   * - Area
     - AR example
     - VR example
   * - Education
     - Point a tablet at a textbook page to see a 3D heart beating
     - Take a virtual excursion to the Great Barrier Reef or ancient Rome
   * - Health
     - Show a surgeon a patient's scan on top of their body
     - Train surgeons safely, or help patients manage pain and fear
   * - Shopping
     - Place a virtual couch in your lounge room to check it fits
     - Walk through a virtual car showroom
   * - Entertainment
     - Face filters, AR games
     - Immersive VR games and concerts
   * - Work and industry
     - Show a mechanic the steps to repair an engine
     - Practise flying a plane in a simulator
   * - Navigation
     - Arrows drawn on the street through your camera
     - Explore a city before you visit

.. admonition:: Safety and wellbeing
   :class: warning

   * Use VR in a clear space and take regular breaks. Some people feel dizzy (motion sickness).
   * Most headset makers recommend a minimum age, often 13 or older. Check before using one.
   * Watch where you are walking when using AR on your phone.
   * AR apps use your camera, so think about privacy. Do not film other people without permission.

Activities
-----------

Activity 1: AR, MR or VR?
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

.. classifying::
   :bins: Augmented reality, Mixed reality, Virtual reality
   :delimiter: =>

   A face filter adds bunny ears in a camera app => Augmented reality
   A phone app shows how a new couch looks in your lounge room => Augmented reality
   Pointing a tablet at a poster makes a dinosaur appear on it => Augmented reality
   A virtual ball bounces off your real table and rolls under your real chair => Mixed reality
   A virtual pet hides behind your real couch => Mixed reality
   You wear a headset and explore a fully computer-made underwater world => Virtual reality
   A pilot practises landing in a full flight simulator headset => Virtual reality
   You play a rhythm game inside a completely virtual concert hall => Virtual reality

Activity 2: Key terms
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

.. gapfill::

   AR adds digital content @@on top of|instead of|behind@@ the real world.
   VR @@replaces|adds to|ignores@@ the real world with a computer-made world.
   An image that an AR app recognises is called a @@marker|headset|plane@@.
   Finding flat surfaces like floors is called @@plane detection|immersion|rendering@@.
   The feeling of being inside a digital world is called @@immersion|tracking|augmentation@@.
   The umbrella term for AR, MR and VR is @@extended reality (XR)|virtual reality (VR)|artificial intelligence (AI)@@.

      [ ] A virtual couch placed on the floor using plane detection | That is markerless AR.
      [ ] A creature appears at a park using GPS | That is location-based AR.

Activity 4: Concept map
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Create a **concept map** with **Extended reality (XR)** in the centre. Add branches for **AR**, **MR**
and **VR**. For each branch, add:

* what it is
* the devices used
* at least two everyday uses
* key terms that belong to it

Draw lines between related ideas and write a word on each line to explain the link (for example,
*AR* → **uses** → *markers*). You can draw it on paper or use a digital tool.

~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Challenge: design an AR idea
-----------------------------

Think of a problem at school that AR could help with (for example, helping new Year 7 students find their
classrooms, or showing how a science experiment works). Write a short paragraph describing:

* who would use it
* what they would see through the camera
* whether it would use a marker or be markerless, and why

Exam-style question
--------------------

.. structuredquestion:: AR and VR
   :total-marks: 6
   :category: Augmented reality

   .. stimulus::

      A museum wants to help visitors learn about dinosaurs. Option A: visitors point their phones at signs
      next to each skeleton to see the dinosaur with skin and moving. Option B: visitors put on a headset
      and walk through a completely virtual prehistoric forest.

   .. subquestion:: a) Identify which option uses AR and which uses VR. Justify your answer.
      :marks: 2

      .. model-answer::

         Option A is AR, because digital content (the moving dinosaur) is added on top of the real view of
         the skeleton. Option B is VR, because the headset replaces the real world with a completely virtual
         forest.

      .. marking-guidance::

         * 1 mark for correctly identifying both.
         * 1 mark for a justification that uses the idea of adding to vs replacing the real world.

   .. subquestion:: b) Option A uses the signs as markers. Explain what a marker is.
      :marks: 1

      .. model-answer::

         A marker is an image that the AR app recognises so it knows where to place the digital content.

      .. marking-guidance::

         1 mark for a correct explanation.

   .. subquestion:: c) Give one advantage and one disadvantage of Option B compared with Option A.
      :marks: 2

      .. model-answer::

         Advantage: VR is more immersive, so visitors feel like they are really in the prehistoric forest.
         Disadvantage: the museum must buy and clean headsets, and visitors cannot see the real exhibits or
         people around them, so they need a safe, clear space.

      .. marking-guidance::

         * 1 mark for a valid advantage.
         * 1 mark for a valid disadvantage.

   .. subquestion:: d) Suggest one other everyday use of AR outside a museum.
      :marks: 1

      .. model-answer::

         Placing virtual furniture in your home before buying it (accept face filters, navigation arrows,
         AR textbooks or similar).

      .. marking-guidance::

         1 mark for any valid everyday use of AR.
