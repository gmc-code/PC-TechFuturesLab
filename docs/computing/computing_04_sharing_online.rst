==========================================
4. Sharing online and the law
==========================================

Learning intentions
--------------------

* I can explain why I should think about what I share online (purposeful sharing).
* I can describe how shared personal information can be used for harmful purposes.
* I can describe my legal responsibilities when I share information and images online.

Success criteria
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* I can sort information into safe to share, share with care, and keep private.
* I can explain what a digital footprint is and why it matters.
* I can name at least three Australian laws or rules about sharing online and say what they do.

Purposeful sharing
-------------------

**Purposeful sharing** means thinking about **why** you are sharing something, **who** will see it
and **what could happen** before you post or send it.

Everything you do online leaves a trail called your **digital footprint**: your posts, photos, comments,
likes, accounts and even searches. Your digital footprint can last for years, and future schools or
employers may look at it.

Before you share, ask yourself **THINK**:

* **T**: Is it **true**?
* **H**: Is it **helpful**?
* **I**: Is it **inspiring**?
* **N**: Is it **necessary**?
* **K**: Is it **kind**, and would I be happy for my family, teachers or a future boss to see it?

Personal information
---------------------

**Personal information** is any information that identifies you or could be used to work out who you are.

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 30 70

   * - Category
     - Examples
   * - Usually safe to share
     - First name, favourite band, opinions about a movie, your artwork (without your full name)
   * - Share with care
     - Photos of your face, your school sports team, your age, your hobbies and the places you
       often go
   * - Keep private
     - Full name with date of birth, home address, phone number, school name and uniform, passwords,
       bank or card details, Medicare number, passport or licence number, your live location

How shared information can be misused
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Criminals and bullies can join small pieces of information together, like a jigsaw puzzle.

* **Identity theft:** Your full name, birthday and address can be used to open accounts or apply
  for loans in your name.
* **Scams and phishing:** Knowing your school, pet's name or favourite team helps a scammer write a
  message that looks real, or guess your security questions.
* **Account hacking:** Many people use pets' names or birthdays as passwords.
* **Stalking:** Photos with your uniform, street signs or location tags can show where you are.
* **Embarrassment and bullying:** A photo or message can be copied, screenshotted and shared
  with people you never meant to see it.

Worked example: the jigsaw
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Alex posts these over a month, each one harmless on its own:

#. A birthday cake photo: "Happy 14th to me! 🎂 12 March"
#. A selfie in school uniform at the front gate
#. "Walking Biscuit 🐶 at the park on Smith St again"
#. "First game for the Under-15s this Saturday at 9!"

From these four posts a stranger could work out Alex's **age and birthday**, **school**,
**street**, **dog's name** (a common password or security answer) and **where Alex will be on
Saturday at 9 am**.

**Better choices:** Keep accounts private, do not show your uniform or street, post about places
after you have left, and never use pets' names or birthdays in passwords.

Your legal responsibilities
----------------------------

Australia has laws about what can be shared online. Breaking them can lead to accounts being removed,
fines or even criminal charges.

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 30 70

   * - Law or rule
     - What it means for you
   * - **Social Media Minimum Age** (Online Safety Act 2021)
     - Since 10 December 2025, age-restricted social media platforms (including Facebook, Instagram,
       Snapchat, TikTok, X, YouTube, Reddit, Threads, Twitch and Kick) must take reasonable steps to
       stop Australians **under 16** from having accounts. The responsibility is on the
       **platforms**, not on young people or parents. Platforms can be fined up to $49.5 million.
   * - **Online Safety Act 2021** and the **eSafety Commissioner**
     - eSafety can order platforms to remove serious cyberbullying of Australian children, image-based
       abuse (sharing intimate images without consent) and illegal content. You can report these at
       `esafety.gov.au <https://www.esafety.gov.au/report>`_.
   * - **Privacy Act 1988**
     - Organisations covered by the Act must follow the **Australian Privacy Principles**: they must
       collect only the personal information they need, keep it secure and tell you how it will be
       used. A **Children's Online Privacy Code** is being made under this Act and is due to be
       registered by **10 December 2026**. It will set stronger privacy rules for online services
       that children use.
   * - **Copyright Act 1968**
     - Photos, music, videos, artwork and writing belong to the person who made them. You need
       permission (or a licence such as Creative Commons) to copy and share them, especially in
       your own projects.
   * - **Criminal Code**
     - Using the internet or a phone to menace, harass or seriously offend someone is a crime.
       Publishing someone's personal details to harass them (called **doxxing**) is also a crime.
       Creating or sharing sexual images of anyone **under 18**, including yourself or a friend,
       is a serious crime.

.. admonition:: If something goes wrong
   :class: important

   * Talk to a trusted adult: a parent, carer, teacher or school counsellor.
   * Take screenshots as evidence (but never screenshot or forward nude images of anyone under 18).
   * Block and report the account on the platform.
   * Report serious cyberbullying or image-based abuse to `eSafety <https://www.esafety.gov.au/report>`_.
   * For support, contact `Kids Helpline <https://kidshelpline.com.au/>`_ on 1800 55 1800 (free, any time).

Activity 4: Quiz
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

.. mcqgroup::

   .. multichoice::

      What is a digital footprint?

      [ ] The size of your phone's storage | That is storage capacity.
      [x] The trail of information you leave online | Correct. Posts, likes, comments and accounts all add to it.
      [ ] A login method using your foot | Not a real login method!
      [ ] A virus that tracks your location | That would be spyware.

   .. multichoice::

      Why is it risky to use your pet's name as a password?

      [ ] Pets' names are too long | Length is not the problem.
      [x] People often share their pets' names online, so they are easy to guess | Correct. Attackers look for this kind of information.
      [ ] Passwords cannot contain names | Passwords can contain names, but guessable ones are weak.
      [ ] Pets' names are against the law | There is no law about this.

   .. multichoice::

      Your friend posts an embarrassing photo of you without asking. What is the best **first** step?

      [ ] Post an embarrassing photo of them back | That makes things worse and could be bullying.
      [x] Ask them to take it down, and talk to a trusted adult if they do not | Correct. Most problems can be fixed this way. If it is serious, report it.
      [ ] Delete your own account | You should not have to leave because of someone else's behaviour.
      [ ] Ignore it forever | It is better to ask for it to be removed and get help.

Challenge: privacy audit
-------------------------

With a parent or carer, check the privacy settings on one app or account your family uses (for example,
a gaming account, a messaging app or a photo app).

#. Who can see your profile and posts?
#. Is your location being shared? With whom?
#. Can strangers message you or add you as a friend?
#. Change one setting to make the account safer, and write down what you changed and why.

Exam-style question
--------------------

.. structuredquestion:: Sharing safely and legally
   :total-marks: 7
   :category: Online safety

   .. stimulus::

      Priya, aged 14, wants to make a video review of a new game and share it in her class group chat. She plans
      to use a popular song as background music, film herself in her school uniform in front of her
      house, and add a caption with her full name.

   .. subquestion:: a) Identify two pieces of personal information Priya would be sharing and explain the risk of each.
      :marks: 4

      .. model-answer::

         * Her school uniform shows which school she goes to, so a stranger could find her there.
         * Filming in front of her house could show her street or house number, so someone could find
           where she lives.
         * Her full name could be used for identity theft or to find her other accounts.

      .. marking-guidance::

         * 1 mark for each correct piece of personal information (maximum 2).
         * 1 mark for each matching risk explained (maximum 2).

   .. subquestion:: b) Explain one legal issue with Priya's plan.
      :marks: 2

      .. model-answer::

         The song is protected by copyright. Using it without permission or a licence could break the
         Copyright Act, and the video could be removed or muted if it is shared online.

      .. marking-guidance::

         * 1 mark for identifying copyright of the song.
         * 1 mark for explaining permission or a licence is needed, or the consequence.

   .. subquestion:: c) Suggest one change that would make Priya's video safer.
      :marks: 1

      .. model-answer::

         Film inside without the uniform and use only her first name (or a nickname). She could also
         use royalty-free music.

      .. marking-guidance::

         1 mark for any sensible change that removes a risk.
