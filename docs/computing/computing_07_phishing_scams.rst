====================================
7. Phishing, scams and cyber threats
====================================

Learning intentions
--------------------

* I can explain what phishing and scams are and how they trick people.
* I can identify the warning signs in a suspicious message.
* I can describe other common cyber threats and what to do if something goes wrong.

Success criteria
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* I can find at least five warning signs in a phishing email or text.
* I can explain the "Stop. Check. Protect." steps.
* I can name where to report a scam or cybercrime in Australia.

Scams in Australia
-------------------

A **scam** is a trick to steal your money or personal information. Scams are big business. In 2025,
Australians reported losing **$2.18 billion** to scams, and **phishing** was the scam type reported
most often to Scamwatch (more than 65 000 reports).

What is phishing?
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

**Phishing** (said "fishing") is when a scammer sends a fake message that pretends to be from a
trusted organisation, such as a bank, delivery company, school, streaming service or government
department. The scammer is "fishing" for your password, bank details or personal information.

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 25 75

   * - Type
     - How it arrives
   * - Phishing
     - Email
   * - Smishing
     - SMS text message
   * - Vishing
     - Voice call (phone)
   * - Spear phishing
     - A message made for **one** person, using details about them (often found on social media)
   * - Quishing
     - A QR code that takes you to a fake website

Warning signs
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Urgency or threats:** "Your account will be closed in 24 hours!"
* **Too good to be true:** "You have won a free iPhone!"
* **Asks for personal details** such as passwords, PINs, card numbers or one-time codes. Real banks
  never ask for these by email or text.
* **Strange sender address:** ``support@paypa1-secure.net`` instead of ``paypal.com``.
* **Links that do not match:** Hover over a link (do not click) to see where it really goes.
* **Generic greeting:** "Dear customer" instead of your name.
* **Spelling and grammar mistakes** (although scams written with AI can look very professional).
* **Unexpected attachments** or requests to install an app.
* **Unusual payment requests:** gift cards, cryptocurrency or "change of bank details".

Worked example: spot the phish
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

.. code-block:: text

   From: AusPost Delivery <notice@auspost-redelivery.info>
   Subject: URGENT: Parcel on hold

   Dear Customer,

   Your parcel could not be delivered due to an incomplete adress.
   Pay the $1.99 redelivery fee within 12 hours or your parcel will be returned.

   Click here: http://auspost-redelivery.info/pay

**Warning signs:**

#. The sender's address is not an official Australia Post address.
#. "URGENT" and a 12-hour deadline create pressure.
#. "Dear Customer" is a generic greeting.
#. "adress" is spelt wrong.
#. It asks for a payment. Paying gives the scammer your card details.
#. The link goes to a strange website.

**What to do:** Do not click. If you are expecting a parcel, open the official app or type the
official website address yourself.

Stop. Check. Protect.
----------------------

Scamwatch tells Australians to follow three steps:

* **Stop:** Do not rush. Scammers create pressure. Never give money or personal details if you are
  unsure.
* **Check:** Ask yourself if the message could be fake. Contact the organisation using a phone number
  or website **you** look up, not the one in the message.
* **Protect:** Act quickly if something goes wrong. Contact your bank, change your passwords and report it.

Other cyber threats
--------------------

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 25 75

   * - Threat
     - What it is
   * - Malware
     - Malicious software. The general name for software designed to cause harm.
   * - Virus
     - Malware that attaches to files and spreads when they are shared.
   * - Ransomware
     - Malware that locks or encrypts your files and demands payment (a ransom) to unlock them.
       Backups protect you.
   * - Spyware
     - Secretly records what you do, such as your keystrokes or location.
   * - Trojan
     - Malware disguised as a useful or fun program, such as a "free" game hack.
   * - Account takeover
     - An attacker logs in as you, often to scam your friends.
   * - Fake online shops
     - Websites with very cheap prices that take your money and send nothing.
   * - Identity theft
     - Using your personal details to pretend to be you.

If something goes wrong
------------------------

#. **Tell a trusted adult** straight away. There is no shame in being scammed. Scammers are professionals.
#. **Contact your bank** immediately if you shared card or bank details.
#. **Change your passwords** (and turn on MFA) for any account that might be affected.
#. **Report it:**

   * Scams: `Scamwatch <https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/report-a-scam>`_
   * Cybercrime (to police): `ReportCyber <https://www.cyber.gov.au/report-and-recover/report>`_
   * Identity theft support: `IDCARE <https://www.idcare.org/>`_ on 1800 595 160
   * Cyberbullying or image-based abuse: `eSafety <https://www.esafety.gov.au/report>`_

Activity 2: Scam or safe?
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

.. classifying::
   :bins: Probably a scam, Probably safe
   :delimiter: =>

   A text says you won a $1000 gift card and must click a link to claim it => Probably a scam
   An email asks you to reply with your password to "verify your account" => Probably a scam
   A message from a "friend" asks you to send the 6-digit code they just sent to your phone => Probably a scam
   A website sells new sneakers for 80% off and only takes gift cards => Probably a scam
   A pop-up says your computer has a virus and to call a number now => Probably a scam
   Your teacher reminds the class about a due date in the school portal => Probably safe
   You open the bank's official app yourself and see a message about new features => Probably safe
   A delivery notice appears in the official courier app for a parcel you ordered => Probably safe

Challenge: make your own (harmless) phish
------------------------------------------

On paper (never send it to anyone), design a fake phishing email for a made-up company. Hide at least
**six** warning signs in it. Swap with a partner and see if they can find them all. Then write a
"how to spot a scam" poster for Year 7 students using what you learned.

Exam-style question
--------------------

.. structuredquestion:: Phishing and scams
   :total-marks: 8
   :category: Cybersecurity

   .. stimulus::

      Ethan receives this text message:

      *"MyGov: Your tax refund of $642.10 is ready. Verify your identity within 24hrs at
      mygov-refunds-au.com to receive payment. Reply STOP to opt out."*

   .. subquestion:: a) What type of attack is this? Explain your answer.
      :marks: 2

      .. model-answer::

         Smishing, because it is a phishing attack sent by SMS text message. It pretends to be a trusted
         government service to trick Ethan into entering personal details.

      .. marking-guidance::

         * 1 mark for smishing (accept phishing).
         * 1 mark for the explanation (fake message by SMS pretending to be trusted).

   .. subquestion:: b) Identify three warning signs in the message.
      :marks: 3

      .. model-answer::

         * The 24-hour deadline creates urgency.
         * The website address is not an official government address (official ones end in ``.gov.au``).
         * It offers money (a refund), which is a common lure.
         * It asks Ethan to verify his identity through a link.

      .. marking-guidance::

         1 mark for each correct warning sign (maximum 3).

   .. subquestion:: c) Ethan already clicked the link and typed in his bank details. Describe three things he should do now.
      :marks: 3

      .. model-answer::

         * Tell a trusted adult.
         * Contact his bank straight away so it can block his card and watch for strange payments.
         * Change his passwords and turn on multi-factor authentication.
         * Report the scam to Scamwatch (and to police through ReportCyber if money was taken).

      .. marking-guidance::

         1 mark for each correct action (maximum 3).
