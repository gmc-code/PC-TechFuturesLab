======================================
3. Data, information and communication
======================================

Learning intentions
--------------------

* I can explain the difference between data and information.
* I can describe how data is collected and turned into information that helps users make decisions.
* I can explain how information systems help people communicate.

Success criteria
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* I can give an example of data being processed into information.
* I can describe the parts of an information system: people, data, processes and digital systems.
* I can explain how an everyday app uses data to inform its users.

Data and information
---------------------

**Data** is raw facts and figures, such as numbers, words, pictures or sounds. On its own, data
often does not mean much.

**Information** is data that has been **processed** (organised, sorted, calculated or displayed)
so that it has **meaning** and is **useful**.

.. code-block:: text

   DATA                    PROCESS                 INFORMATION
   18, 21, 24, 19, 17  →   find the average,   →   "The average temperature this
   (temperatures)          put on a graph          week was 19.8 °C. Wednesday
                                                   was the hottest day."

Worked example: a weather app
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

#. **Collect data:** Weather stations measure temperature, rainfall and wind speed every few minutes.
#. **Store data:** The readings are saved in a database.
#. **Process data:** Computers average the readings, compare them to past years and run forecast models.
#. **Present information:** The app shows "Tomorrow: 28 °C, 70% chance of rain" with an icon.
#. **Make a decision:** You decide to take an umbrella.

The user never sees the millions of numbers. They see the **information** they need to make a
decision. This is how **data informs the user**.

More examples
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 25 35 40

   * - System
     - Data collected
     - Information for the user
   * - Fitness watch
     - Steps, heart rate, GPS points
     - "You walked 9200 steps today, 20% more than yesterday."
   * - School portal
     - Assessment marks
     - Your grade and a chart of your progress this year
   * - Maps app
     - Location of thousands of phones
     - "Heavy traffic. Your trip will take 12 extra minutes."
   * - Streaming service
     - What you watched, paused and skipped
     - "Because you watched..." recommendations
   * - Shop checkout
     - Every item scanned
     - Which products sell best, when to reorder stock

Information systems
--------------------

An **information system** is a combination of four parts working together:

* **People:** the users and the people who build and run the system
* **Data:** the facts that go into the system
* **Processes:** the steps that turn data into information (collecting, storing, sorting, calculating,
  sharing)
* **Digital systems:** the hardware, software and networks

When any part goes wrong, the whole system can fail. For example, if people type in the wrong data,
the information that comes out will be wrong too. Programmers call this **"garbage in, garbage out"**.

Information systems for communication
--------------------------------------

Information systems help people **communicate** across distance and time.

* **Video calls** let families in different countries see and talk to each other for free.
* **Messaging apps** send text, photos and voice messages instantly.
* **Email** lets schools and businesses send detailed messages and files.
* **Learning platforms** let students and teachers share work, feedback and notices.
* **Emergency alert apps** send warnings about fires and floods to everyone in an area.
* **Social networking** lets people share news and stay in touch. (In Australia, platforms such as
  Instagram, Snapchat and TikTok must now take reasonable steps to stop under-16s from having
  accounts. You will learn more about this in :doc:`computing_04_sharing_online`.)

.. admonition:: Think about it
   :class: note

   A computer can be a phone, a camera, a TV and a post office all at once. Before smartphones, people
   needed a separate device for each of these jobs.

Activity 2: Put the steps in order
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Put the steps a maps app uses to warn you about traffic into the correct order.

.. ordering::
   :no-indent:

   Phones in cars send their location and speed to the maps company.
   The location data is stored on the company's servers.
   Computers calculate the average speed on each road.
   Roads where cars are moving slowly are marked red on the map.
   The app tells you there is heavy traffic and suggests a faster route.
   You decide to take the other route.

Activity 4: Quiz
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

.. mcqgroup::

   .. multichoice::

      What turns data into information?

      [ ] Deleting it | Deleting data destroys it.
      [x] Processing it so it has meaning | Correct. Sorting, calculating and displaying data gives it meaning.
      [ ] Storing it on a hard drive | Storing keeps data, but does not give it meaning.
      [ ] Collecting more of it | More data is still data until it is processed.

   .. multichoice::

      A teacher types a student's mark as 9 instead of 90. The report shows the student failed.
      Which idea does this show?

      [x] Garbage in, garbage out | Correct. Wrong data in gives wrong information out.
      [ ] Encryption | Encryption scrambles data to protect it.
      [ ] Volatile memory | Volatile memory is about losing data when the power goes off.
      [ ] Biometrics | Biometrics are about checking who someone is.

   .. multichoice::

      Which is **not** one of the four parts of an information system?

      [ ] People | People are part of every information system.
      [ ] Processes | Processes turn data into information.
      [x] Profits | Correct. Profit is not one of the four parts.
      [ ] Digital systems | Hardware, software and networks are the digital systems.

Exam-style question
--------------------

.. structuredquestion:: Data informing users
   :total-marks: 6
   :category: Data and information

   .. stimulus::

      A school canteen uses an online ordering system. Students order lunch on an app before 9 am.
      The system records every order. At the end of each week, the canteen manager sees a report
      that lists the five most popular meals and how much food was thrown away.

   .. subquestion:: a) Using the canteen system, give one example of data and one example of information.
      :marks: 2

      .. model-answer::

         Data: each individual order (for example, "Student 1043 ordered a chicken wrap").
         Information: the weekly report of the five most popular meals.

      .. marking-guidance::

         * 1 mark for a correct example of raw data.
         * 1 mark for a correct example of processed information.

   .. subquestion:: b) Describe how the canteen manager could use the report to make a decision.
      :marks: 2

      .. model-answer::

         The manager could make more of the popular meals and less of meals that are often thrown
         away. This reduces waste and saves money.

      .. marking-guidance::

         * 1 mark for a sensible decision.
         * 1 mark for linking the decision to information in the report.

   .. subquestion:: c) Explain what could happen if students entered the wrong data into the system.
      :marks: 2

      .. model-answer::

         The report would be wrong ("garbage in, garbage out"). For example, if students ordered the
         wrong meal by mistake, the manager might make too much of a meal nobody really wants, so
         more food would be wasted.

      .. marking-guidance::

         * 1 mark for saying the information or report would be wrong.
         * 1 mark for a realistic consequence.
